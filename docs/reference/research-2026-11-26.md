# Research round, 26 November 2026: sector rules, US states and international instruments

Prompted by a list of about 200 AI policies that a reader tracks. About 60 were already on record. This round
added 96 instruments and five US state jurisdictions (Arizona, Indiana, New Jersey, North Dakota,
Pennsylvania), all `pending_review`.

## How the facts were gathered (read before reviewing)

The research environment could not open official pages: legislature, regulator, gazette and government hosts
were refused by its network policy. Every fact comes from search-engine extracts of the official URLs
recorded on each instrument, as in the [15 October round](subnational-research-2026-10-15.md). So:

- every new record and jurisdiction is `pending_review`, confidence `medium` at most (`low` where the
  extracts were thin, conflicting or only a press release);
- a date that was not confirmed is left null and explained in `date_notes`; a date the reader's list gave
  but no official extract confirmed was not used;
- nothing rests on secondary sources alone; where enactment was shown only by secondary reporting the record
  was held back (below);
- summaries are written fresh from the official extracts. The reader's list came from a commercial
  database and was used only as a list of names to look for.

General laws that also govern AI (GDPR, UK GDPR, APPI, FCRA, ECOA, 42 CFR Part 2, NIST SP 800-53 and others)
are recorded with `ai_specific: false`.

## Second round, 26 November 2026

A follow-up round researched the items the first round's search allowance did not reach, and re-checked the two held
bills against official records. It added 33 policy records, seven standards (metadata only, in `data/implementation/`),
six controls, and updated three existing records. Same method and limits as above; everything is `pending_review`.

**Updated records.** Taiwan's Artificial Intelligence Basic Act moves from the 2024 Executive Yuan draft to the act passed
on 23 December 2025 and promulgated on 14 January 2026 (in force that day); its earlier verification covered the draft
only, so it is pending review again. The EU Article 50 code-of-practice placeholder is filled (published 10 June 2026),
with change events for the code, the Commission's Article 50 guidelines (20 July 2026) and Taiwan. Singapore's agentic
AI framework records its May 2026 update (IMDA does not call it version 1.5).

**Held bills, now confirmed.** Alabama SB 63 is Act 2026-589, approved 16 April 2026 (secondary sources said 17 April);
Indiana HB 1271 is Public Law 88, signed 4 March 2026.

**Where official sources disagreed with the list:** Minnesota's privacy act is codified at Minn. Stat. 325M.10 to
325M.21, not chapter 325O; New York Civil Rights Law 52-c dates from 2020 (chapter 304), not 2023; Israel's programme
rests on Government Decision 212 of 1 August 2021 and a separate 2026 decision, not one 2021–2026 instrument.

**Not recorded:** SAMA (no AI-specific instrument found; the Counter-Fraud Framework only mentions AI); the TRUMP AMERICA
AI Act (still no bill introduced). Leads: Israel Government Decision 4255 (June 2026); a February 2026 SEBI master
circular with AI disclosure rules for investment advisers; the ONC rule of 28 October 2026 amending HTI-1.

**Verification budget.** With the second round, `policy:freshness` counts 191 critical records never confirmed
(budget 98): 21 more binding records, plus Taiwan's act, which is pending review again.

**New controls** (original, pending review). Published, each linked to a pending obligation: crisis detection and
referral for conversational AI (New York GBL 1701), and age assurance and protective defaults for minors (Washington ESHB
2225). Drafted but unpublished until an obligation they serve is recorded (the site publishes no control nothing
references): independent bias audit, qualified human review of AI-assisted coverage and clinical decisions, a consent and
licence register for digital replicas, and an AI safety reporting channel. The bias audit and reporting controls fit the
verified NYC Local Law 144 and SB 53 obligations; linking them is left to a reviewer, since those records are verified.
